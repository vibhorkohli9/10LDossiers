SEBI SETTLEMENT ORDER (Feb 2021) ▶
SEBI | Adjudicating Officer: G. Ramar | Date: 9 February 2021
"Pending adjudication proceedings, the noticee (InterGlobe Aviation) proposed to settle the instant proceedings initiated against it, without admitting or denying the findings of fact and conclusions of law, through a settlement order."

Key findings listed in order:

1. EY report on RPTs commissioned by Chairman M. Damodaran was not shared with the board or audit committee. Only Damodaran saw it; findings communicated to board in March 2019. Gangwal made multiple requests — never shared.

2. Related-party transaction with InterGlobe Air Transport Ltd (ticketing vendor) — Rs 299.24 Cr as of 31 Mar 2019. IndiGo claimed this was not an RPT. SEBI disagreed; held audit committee approval was required.

3. Lease agreements with Pegasus Buildtech Pvt. Ltd and Caddie Hotels Pvt. Ltd — audit committee nod obtained post-facto in some cases.

4. Prospectus (16 Oct 2015) stated RoFR would cease post 10 Nov 2019. Since shareholders did not vote for change, agreement continued. SEBI held this as misrepresentation / violation of disclosure norms.

5. EGM sought by Gangwal in May 2019 to enforce governance standards — never convened. Legal opinion communicated "mere six minutes before" scheduled board meeting.

Settlement amount: Rs 2.10 Crore.

LCIA FINAL AWARD (Sep 2021) ▶
London Court of International Arbitration | Award Date: 23 September 2021
"Claimants [Respondents] and Respondents 1-3 [Petitioners] shall jointly take the following actions to remove the Transfer Restriction Articles from the Company's Articles of Association: (i) make a requisition to call an extraordinary general meeting of shareholders to vote on a resolution to remove the Transfer Restriction Articles... within 30 days after the date of this Award."

The tribunal ordered removal of RoFR and tag-along clauses from the AoA. Both sides required to split costs equally, vote in favour, and convene EGM within statutory timelines.

Delhi High Court (Justice Sanjeev Narula, 8 Oct 2021) enforced this under Section 9 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, appointing a Local Commissioner to supervise the EGM.

GANGWAL'S SEBI COMPLAINT (Jul 2019) ▶
49-Page Document Filed with SEBI | 8 July 2019 Letter

Core allegations:

1. RPT Concerns: Related-party transactions between IndiGo and Bhatia-linked IGE Group entities grew from Rs 31 Cr (FY2010) to ~10x by FY2018. Areas: ticketing, simulation training, crew accommodation, leasing.

2. Governance Failure: Bhatia's "unusual controlling rights" allowed him to push through decisions without meaningful checks. Board appointments lacked independence.

3. Prospectus Misrepresentation: RoFR rights described as ceasing post-Nov 2019, but no action taken to remove from AoA.

4. Material Non-Disclosure: Failure to make timely disclosure of information likely to have significant market reaction.

5. EGM Blocked: Request for EGM to adopt new RPT protocols — blocked by IGE Group's 38% shareholding (special resolution requiring 75%).

The Gray Zone: Nobody Was Wrong

Bhatia was not wrong to structure the SHA the way he did. The IGE Group took disproportionate economic risk (Rs 1,100+ Cr at peak). Control rights were the price of that risk. Gangwal signed the agreement in 2006, participated in the 2015 IPO where the same structure was disclosed, and approved annual accounts for 13 years. Silence is not fraud. Consent is not coercion.

Gangwal was not wrong to raise governance concerns when RPT values grew 10x and the EY report was withheld from the full board. A shareholder who discovers that audit committee approvals were obtained post-facto, that a ticketing vendor transaction worth Rs 299 Cr was not treated as an RPT, and that the Chairman alone saw a critical review — that shareholder is not a troublemaker. He is a fiduciary.

SEBI was not wrong to list the lapses in its settlement order, even while accepting a no-fault settlement. The regulator's job is to protect market integrity, not to pick sides in a marital dispute between billionaires.

The LCIA tribunal was not wrong to order removal of Transfer Restriction Articles. Both founders had locked each other in a cage of their own design. The tribunal simply unlocked the door.

The only wrong was the expectation that two people who built a fortress together would agree on who owns the keys.
